# Adaptive dyadic divisor-lift conjecture

**Status:** exact algebraic family + finite evidence; universal existence is conjectural  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Depends on:** `FAB-RECIPROCAL-DUALITY.md`, `FAB-RECIPROCAL-SIGNED-TARGET.md`, `FAB-RECIPROCAL-CHARACTER-TRANSPORT.md`

## 1. Adaptive parameter from p-1

Let `p` be a Mordell-hard prime. Then

\[
p\equiv1\pmod{24},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{V=\frac{p-1}{2}}
\]

is divisible by `12`.

Choose any positive divisor

\[
\boxed{t\mid V}
\]

and put

\[
\boxed{d_t=4t-1.}
\]

Then `d_t=3 mod4`, so both the forward and reciprocal fixed-`d_t` fab criteria are available.

This is adaptive: the candidate moduli are generated by the divisor structure of the input prime itself.

## 2. Exact factor injection

Write

\[
\boxed{V=ts,}
\]

so

\[
p=2ts+1.
\]

The paired bases become

\[
X_t
:=\frac{p+d_t}{4}
=\frac{t(s+2)}2,
\]

and

\[
Y_t
:=\frac{pd_t+1}{4}
=\frac{t(d_t s+2)}2.
\]

These are integers because `ts=V` is divisible by `4`.

Let

\[
\epsilon=\gcd(t,2),
\qquad
\boxed{g=t/\epsilon.}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{g\mid X_t,
\qquad g\mid Y_t.}
\]

More explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
X_t
=g\cdot\frac{\epsilon(s+2)}2,
\qquad
Y_t
=g\cdot\frac{\epsilon(d_t s+2)}2.
}
\]

Thus every divisor `t|(p-1)/2` injects a known factor into **both** fab lanes before any search is performed.

## 3. Why the injected factor is on the residue side

Every prime factor `r|(p-1)` satisfies

\[
p\equiv1\pmod r.
\]

Since `p=1 mod4`, quadratic reciprocity gives

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac r p\right)=+1}
\]

for every odd prime `r|(p-1)`; the factor `2` is also a residue because `p=1 mod8`.

Hence every prime factor of the injected factor `g` is a quadratic residue modulo `p`.

When `d_t` is squarefree, character transport also gives

\[
\left(\frac r{d_t}\right)=+1
\]

for those factors as they occur inside `X_t` or `Y_t`.

The adaptive family therefore seeds the positive-character side from `p-1`; a successful lane must obtain its genuinely new negative-character information from the moving complementary factors.

## 4. The first fixed moduli reappear structurally

Because every Mordell-hard prime has

\[
12\mid V,
\]

the divisors

\[
t=1,2,3,4,6,12
\]

are universally available. They give

\[
\boxed{d_t=3,7,11,15,23,47.}
\]

Thus several of the strongest small fixed parameters in the reciprocal-double-sieve experiment are not arbitrary successful constants. They are the first nodes of the divisor lattice of `(p-1)/2`.

Additional factors of `p-1` automatically create additional candidate moduli.

## 5. Adaptive dyadic divisor-lift conjecture

The current universal target is:

### Conjecture

For every Mordell-hard prime `p`, there exists

\[
\boxed{t\mid\frac{p-1}{2}}
\]

such that, with

\[
d=4t-1,
\]

at least one of the exact square-divisor conditions holds:

### Forward

\[
\boxed{
\exists D\mid\left(\frac{p+d}{4}\right)^2:
\quad4D\equiv-1\pmod d,
}
\]

or

### Reciprocal

\[
\boxed{
\exists D\mid\left(\frac{pd+1}{4}\right)^2:
\quad4D\equiv-1\pmod d.
}
\]

Either condition gives an explicit exact fab certificate and therefore an Erdős–Straus decomposition for `p`.

This is a cap-free statement. The size and number of candidate parameters grow with the divisor structure of the input rather than being frozen in advance.

## 6. Why the factor 2 matters

The superficially similar family

\[
t\mid\frac{p-1}{4}
\]

is **false** as a finite-existence conjecture even very low in the search.

Exact failures below two million include

\[
214729,
297049,
878089,
1511449.
\]

All four are immediately rescued by

\[
t=4,
\qquad d=15.
\]

For each of them, `(p-1)/4` has only one factor of `2`, so `4` is absent from the smaller divisor lattice but present in `(p-1)/2`.

Thus the passage

\[
\frac{p-1}{4}
\longrightarrow
\frac{p-1}{2}
\]

is not cosmetic. It is the first exact dyadic lift required by the data.

## 7. Exact finite signal through fifty million

An exact finite replay over the six Mordell-hard residue classes modulo `840` gives:

- prime bound: `50,000,000`;
- hard primes: `93,457`;
- candidate rule: `t | (p-1)/2`;
- captured: `93,457`;
- unresolved: `0`;
- largest observed **first-success** `t`: `38`;
- corresponding modulus: `d=151`.

The record finite specimen is

\[
\boxed{p=42,486,889,
\qquad t=38,
\qquad d=151,}
\]

where the reciprocal lane is the first successful adaptive node.

Its reciprocal base is

\[
\boxed{
Y_{151}
=
\frac{151p+1}{4}
=
1,603,880,060
}
\]

with factorization

\[
\boxed{
Y_{151}
=2^2\cdot5\cdot19\cdot71\cdot59447.
}
\]

For this `p`, the first three prime factors `2,5,19` are quadratic residues modulo `p`, while

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac{71}{p}\right)
=
\left(\frac{59447}{p}\right)
=-1.
}
\]

The successful square divisor can be chosen as

\[
\boxed{D=11,889,400=2^3\cdot5^2\cdot59447,}
\]

which satisfies

\[
\boxed{D\mid Y_{151}^2,
\qquad4D\equiv-1\pmod{151}.}
\]

This specimen exhibits the intended mechanism directly: the divisor of `p-1` supplies residue-side structure, and a moving cofactor imports the external nonresidue needed to hit the fixed signed target.

## 8. Higher-range falsification pressure

Random exact tests on hard primes far above the 50M exhaustive cohort have not yet produced a failure of the adaptive rule, but random evidence is not promoted to a theorem here.

The correct research use of the computation is adversarial:

1. search specifically for a prime surviving all small divisor nodes `t|(p-1)/2`;
2. if one is found, inspect the factorization pattern of `(p-1)/2` and the signed-reach defects at every divisor node;
3. if no failure appears under aggressive search, attack the universal statement by a divisor-lattice descent rather than by increasing a fixed `m` bound.

## 9. Proof target

A proof would follow from a theorem of the form:

> Let `p` be Mordell-hard and put `V=(p-1)/2`. Among the divisor nodes `t|V`, at least one paired base `X_t` or `Y_t` has a bounded signed divisor product equal to the fixed target `-4^{-1} mod (4t-1)`.

The known factor `g=t/gcd(t,2)` and the pair symmetry should be treated as structural input, not merely as computational acceleration.
