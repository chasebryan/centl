# ES+ — the letter equation

**Status:** exact dual of the proved prime two-target criterion; a search principle, not a proof  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Program:** ES+  
**Depends on:** `ES-TWO-TARGET-SIGNED-BOX-EQUIVALENCE.md`, `HARD-SMOOTH-TYPEII-OBSTRUCTION.md`, `COMPOSITE-CORE.md`, `ERDOS-STRAUS-WALL.md`  
**Claim boundary:** This note names one set and one generating map. It does not prove Erdős–Straus. It does not prove that the letter spectrum is empty. A nonempty infinite letter spectrum would be a family of primes that miss every admissible signed box up to the bound; that is a computational letter, not a counterexample, until the bound is infinite. No literature-priority claim is made.

---

## 1. What a letter is

A **LETTER** in the existing hunt is a Mordell-hard prime that survives the whole forward menu: the linear theorems, the coprime window \(a,b\le 11\), and the two-target corridor through a finite \(K\). GREAT is the complementary event — the same prime is solved, usually already in the window — and is too common to store.

So a letter is not a new Diophantine object. It is a prime that lies outside a finite union of explicit hitting sets. The forward kernels (bb, CC, CBAP) test membership in that union one prime at a time. That is the wrong orientation if the goal is to *find* letters quickly.

The right orientation is the dual of the boxed two-target theorem.

---

## 2. The signed box, recalled

Let \(p\) be prime, \(p\equiv 1\pmod 4\). Let \(k\) satisfy

\[
k\equiv 3\pmod 4,
\qquad
\gcd(k,p)=1.
\]

Put

\[
C_k=\frac{p+k}{4}
\]

and write \(C_k=\prod_i r_i^{e_i}\). The signed box is

\[
\mathcal R_k(C_k)
=
\Bigl\{
\prod_i r_i^{z_i}\pmod k
:
-e_i\le z_i\le e_i
\Bigr\}
\subseteq(\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]

The two-target theorem already in the repository states

\[
p\text{ has a Type I or Type II solution at shift }k
\iff
\bigl\{-p^{-1},\,-1\bigr\}
\cap
\mathcal R_k(C_k)
\ne\varnothing.
\]

That is an if and only if for that \(k\). Therefore the set of primes that *miss every* admissible \(k\le K\) is exactly the complement of the union of the hitting sets of those \(k\). Complements of unions are generated by marking hits, not by searching.

## 3. Both targets are conditions on \(C\) alone

Fix \(k\) first. Then \(p=4C-k\), and

\[
p\equiv 4C\pmod k.
\]

Since \(k\) is odd, \(\gcd(p,k)=1\) if and only if \(\gcd(C,k)=1\). In that case

\[
4^{-1}\equiv\frac{k+1}{4}\pmod k,
\qquad
p^{-1}\equiv 4^{-1}\,C^{-1}\pmod k.
\]

The two targets become functions of \(C\):

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{II}}=-1\pmod k,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{I}}(C)
=
-4^{-1}C^{-1}
=
-\frac{k+1}{4}\,C^{-1}
\pmod k.
\]

Define the **vacancy** of \(C\) at \(k\) by

\[
\delta_k(C)
=
\begin{cases}
0
&
\text{if }
\gcd(C,k)=1
\text{ and }
\bigl\{\tau_{\mathrm{I}}(C),\,\tau_{\mathrm{II}}\bigr\}
\cap
\mathcal R_k(C)
\ne\varnothing,\\[1em]
1
&
\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]

Then \(p=4C-k\) is a signed-box hit at this \(k\) if and only if \(\delta_k(C)=0\) and \(p\) is an admissible prime. No property of \(p\) remains except primality of \(4C-k\).

That is the inversion.

## 4. The inverse signed-box cover

Let \(\mathcal A_K\) be the admissible shifts of bound \(K\):

\[
\mathcal A_K
=
\{k\le K:k\equiv 3\pmod 4\}.
\]

The **inverse signed-box cover** is the set of all integers generated as hits:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_K
=
\bigcup_{k\in\mathcal A_K}
\Bigl\{
4C-k
:
C\ge 1,\ 
\delta_k(C)=0
\Bigr\}.
}
\]

Let \(H\) be the six Mordell-hard classes

\[
H=\{1,121,169,289,361,529\}\subset\mathbb Z/840\mathbb Z.
\]

The **letter spectrum** of bound \(K\) is

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_K
=
\bigl\{
p\text{ prime}:
p\bmod 840\in H
\bigr\}
\setminus
\mathcal C_K
\setminus
W_K.
}
\]

Here \(W_K\) is the (much cheaper) inverted window: every prime produced by \(4p+1\), by \(p+4\), or by a coprime pair \(fab(a,b)\) with \(a,b\le 11\). Those are the GREAT primes. They are removed because they are not letters. Their generating maps are elementary and are written in the appendix; they are not the new object.

The **ES+ letter equation** is the membership form of the same set:

\[
\boxed{
p\in\Lambda_K
\iff
p\text{ is a Mordell-hard prime}
\text{ and }
\delta_k\Bigl(\tfrac{p+k}{4}\Bigr)=1
\text{ for every }
k\in\mathcal A_K
\text{ with }
\gcd(k,p)=1,
\text{ and }
p\notin W_K.
}
\]

The first display is how one *constructs* \(\Lambda_K\). The second is how one *recognizes* a single \(p\). They are the same set. The first is the algorithm.

---

## 5. Why this is the optimal search

Forward search, for each hard prime \(p\le X\), factors \((p+k)/4\) for many \(k\) and walks a fab table. The cost is

\[
\asymp
\pi_H(X)\cdot\bigl(|\mathcal A_K|+|fab|\bigr)
\]

factorizations, and almost every prime is discarded as GREAT.

Inverse search enumerates, for each \(k\le K\), those \(C\le(X+k)/4\) with \(\delta_k(C)=0\), writes \(p=4C-k\), and marks \(p\) if it is a hard prime. After the union is built, the unmarked hard primes \(\le X\) *are* \(\Lambda_K\cap[1,X]\).

Two facts make this complete and not a heuristic.

1. The two-target theorem is an equivalence. Every Type I or Type II hit at shift \(k\) arises from some \(C\) with \(\delta_k(C)=0\). No letter is lost, and no non-hit is marked by \(\mathcal C_K\).
2. \(\delta_k\) does not depend on an outer loop over primes. The signed box of \(C\) is a finite multiplicative set of size at most \(\prod_i(2e_i+1)\). Smooth \(C\) are cheap; prime \(C\) give a box of three units \(\{C^{\pm 1},1\}\) and are decided by two comparisons.

The hard-smooth Type-II obstruction already in the repository says that no single \(\{2,3,5,7\}\)-smooth multiplier can carry \(\tau_{\mathrm{II}}\) on every hard class. Therefore \(\mathcal C_K\) cannot collapse to one modulus. The union over \(k\) is essential. ES+ does not look for a miracle covering residue. It *builds the covering set that the equivalence already names*, then reads the complement.

If Erdős–Straus holds for primes, then

\[
\bigcap_K\Lambda_K=\varnothing.
\]

That is the wall, restated as a nested sequence of complements. Finite emptiness of \(\Lambda_K\cap[1,X]\) is finite coverage, not a proof. Nonemptiness of some \(\Lambda_K\cap[1,X]\) is a letter: an unsolved hard prime at that bound, with the same content-addressed identity as `ES-LETTER-v1`.

## 6. What is new here, and what is not

Not new: the signed box, the two targets, the six hard classes, the window \(a,b\le 11\), the fact that GREAT is common and letters are rare.

New as an organizing object: **the letter spectrum is the complement of an inversely generated cover**. The hunt should not ask, for each prime, “does some \(k\) hit?”. It should ask, for each admissible \(k\), “which primes does this \(k\) generate?”, and keep only what no \(k\le K\) generated.

CBAP channel D evaluates \(\delta_k((p+k)/4)\) forward until the first zero, then drops. ES+ evaluates the same \(\delta_k\) on \(C\), emits \(4C-k\), and never visits the dropped prime as a search subject. That is the whole gain.

## Appendix. The window set \(W_K\)

These maps are classical and cheap. They are recorded so that \(\Lambda_K\) is exactly the hunt’s LETTER, not a weaker residual.

- \(4p+1\). If \(4p+1=FG\) with \(F\equiv G\equiv 3\pmod 4\), then \(p\) is marked.
- \(p+4\). If \(p+4\) has a divisor \(q\equiv 3\pmod 4\), the standard linear identity marks \(p\).
- \(fab(a,b)\) for coprime \(1\le a,b\le 11\). There exists \(k\mid(a+bp)\) with \(k\equiv -p\pmod{4ab}\). Equivalently, ranging over such \(k\) and writing \(p=(kq-a)/b\) when this is an integer prime marks the same set.

Corridor companions already proved at \(k=3,7,11,15,19\) sit inside \(\mathcal C_K\) as soon as \(K\ge 19\). They do not need a separate clause.

Erdős–Straus remains open. \(\Lambda_K=\varnothing\) on a finite initial segment is not a proof. The equation above is the thing to implement.
