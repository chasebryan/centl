# What W is actually doing

**Status:** finite census of the three W clauses; cbis.kernel was not edited  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Program:** ES+  
**Tool:** `w_census.c` in this directory  

W is not a single test. It is a union. The run that marks every
Mordell-hard prime is three covers stacked. Splitting them is the
thing that can be learned. Do not weaken W.

---

## 1. The three clauses, as arithmetic

A hard prime `p` (so `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`) is marked by W if any one holds.

**4p+1.** Hits iff `4p+1` has a prime factor `≡ 3 (mod 4)`. Then it
splits as `F·G` with `F ≡ G ≡ 3 (mod 4)` and the standard linear
identity applies. Fails iff every prime factor of `4p+1` is `≡ 1
(mod 4)`.

**p+4.** Hits iff `p+4` has a prime factor `≡ 3 (mod 4)`. Fails iff
every prime factor of `p+4` is `≡ 1 (mod 4)`.

**fab.** Coprime `a,b ≤ 11` and a divisor `k` of `a+bp` with
`p+k ≡ 0 (mod 4ab)`.

So the only primes that can possibly leave W are those for which
**both** `4p+1` and `p+4` live in the multiplicative semigroup
generated by primes `≡ 1 (mod 4)`. Call that set

    R = { hard primes p : 4p+1 and p+4 are both 1-mod-4-smooth }.

A W-survivor is exactly an element of `R` that also evades every
fab pair. A LETTER at the window layer has to sit in that residual.
It cannot sit anywhere else.

## 2. Census (independent of cbis)

`w_census` walks Mordell-hard primes and records which clause fired.
Identities `4xyz = p(yz+xz+xy)` were checked on the first 200 linear
hits in each run: zero failures.

| interval | hard primes | W miss | 4p+1 | p+4 | both linears miss (then fab) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| (0, 2e6] | 4519 | 0 | 1917 | 1865 | 1488 |
| (0, 2e7] | 39391 | 0 | 18434 | 17525 | 11291 |
| (0, 1e8] | 179468 | 0 | 87573 | 84010 | 47802 |

Through `10^8`:

- about 49% hit 4p+1
- about 47% hit p+4
- about 27% miss both linears (`R`)
- **every** prime in `R` was then hit by fab
- first fab-only prime: 2521
- 9658489 is in `R` and is a fab hit (cbap solved it by corridor[7];
  W still marks it, by a different construction)

The six hard classes are even. No class is doing the killing alone.

Your 31.5M hard primes with zero W survivors is the same pattern at
larger scale: `R` is real and large, and fab is finishing `R` in the
range you have reached.

---

## 3. What this is not

It is not a bug in the matrix. I and N and L stay at zero because W
is a complete cover of the sampled initial segment. They will move
when a prime in `R` evades every `a,b ≤ 11`.

It is not a reason to drop a clause of W. Each clause is a genuine
identity. Removing fab would flood I with GREATs. Removing a linear
clause would only make W slower.

It is not a theorem that fab `≤ 11` covers all of `R`. The census is
finite. The first W-survivor, if it exists, is the first window-layer
letter.

---

## 4. Direction

Keep W. Change *where you look*.

1. **Instrument the split, don't flatten it.** The useful dashboard
   line is not `W[A108 B113 C111]`. It is
   `linear / R / fab-on-R`. That is the structure the census found.
2. **Letters live in `R`.** Next engine work (new binary, not a
   rewrite of cbis) should generate or walk only primes with
   `4p+1` and `p+4` supported on primes `≡ 1 (mod 4)`, then invert
   fab on that set. That is the W-layer letter equation.
3. **cbis can keep hunting.** A W-survivor at `10^10` or later is
   still a letter under the present stamp. The matrix is waiting in
   the right place. I/N/L are the reserve once `R` minus fab is
   nonempty.

The learned object is `R`, not a weaker W.
