# Audit of Bradford 2026 Claimed ES Solution

**Status:** internal mathematical audit / prior-art calibration  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Source:** Kyle Bradford, *A solution to the Straus-Erdős conjecture*, arXiv:2602.11774v1 (2026).  
**Claim boundary:** this note does not assert that every displayed Type I/II identity in the paper is wrong. It identifies that the paper does not prove the global covering step required for its headline claim.

---

## 1. What the paper does provide

The paper separates prime solutions into Type I and Type II and derives parametric congruence families.

For Type I it gives, for integers `k>=0` and suitable `ell`, congruence classes of primes for which the displayed three-term unit-fraction identity is valid.

For Type II it gives the symmetric family in which the prime lies in a different residue class generated by `-(4k+3)`.

These families may be useful as explicit solution classes and are compatible with the classical Type I/II framework.

---

## 2. The missing theorem is exactly the global theorem

After displaying the first examples for `k=0`, the paper states:

> “The last thing that we must show is that this is a covering system.”

It then observes that `5,13,17,29` are the first four primes congruent to `1 mod 4` and immediately proceeds to the references.

There is no subsequent theorem, induction, density argument, modular-cover certificate, or structural proof that the union over all allowed `(k,ell)` covers every prime

\[
p\equiv1\pmod4.
\]

That missing coverage implication is precisely the statement required to conclude Erdős-Straus from the preceding sufficient congruence families.

---

## 3. Why examples for k=0 are not a covering proof

The paper lists classes such as

\[
p\equiv29,41\pmod{44},
\]

\[
p\equiv13,17\pmod{20},
\]

and further examples modulo `8` and `140`.

Each displayed identity can establish solvability **inside that class** when its denominator-integrality conditions hold.

But a collection of examples is not enough to prove

\[
\boxed{
\bigcup_{k,\ell}\mathcal R_{k,\ell}
\supseteq
\{p:p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv1\pmod4\}.
}
\]

The omitted step is especially nontrivial because classical quadratic-reciprocity obstructions are known to prevent naive finite congruence families from eliminating all square-like residue classes.

---

## 4. Responsible classification

FCF/CENTL should classify the paper as:

```text
explicit Type I / Type II congruence families: useful / independently checkable
claimed global covering system: not proved in the deposited manuscript
headline solution of Erdős-Straus: NOT ACCEPTED on the current text
```

This is an incompleteness finding, not a claim that no completion of the approach is possible.

---

## 5. Relation to the current program

The useful mathematical content belongs naturally in the same language as:

- Elsholtz-Tao Type I/II congruence parametrizations;
- the complete 2026 `fab` divisor parametrization;
- FCF collective-core and exact covering certificates.

Any Bradford-style global proof would need to supply the object absent from the manuscript: an exact universal covering theorem.

Given `DSC-COUNTEREXAMPLE.md`, FCF should be particularly strict here. Collective coverage is a real arithmetic phenomenon, but it must be proved by an actual core/cover argument rather than inferred from the existence of many congruence families.

---

## 6. Salvage target

Instead of treating the manuscript as a completed solution, extract its two parametric residue families and compare them against the exact coprime-`fab` and Type I/II parametrizations.

If they are specializations of those complete identities, they can be incorporated as convenient low-complexity certificate families without inheriting the unsupported covering claim.
