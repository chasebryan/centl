# FCF and CENTL name and branding policy

The Apache License 2.0 and Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 grants in this repository do not grant a general license to use project names, logos, mascots, or distinctive branding as the identity of another product, service, organization, or distribution.

This policy applies, to the extent protected by applicable law, to identifiers including **Free Computation Foundation**, **FCF**, **CENTL**, **CENTL-SCi**, **CENTL Physics**, **CENTL-MIRAGE**, **CENTL CARAVAN**, and their associated logos, camel marks, release artwork, and other distinctive project branding.

## Uses that do not require separate permission

You may use the names in ordinary, accurate reference to the project. Examples include:

- saying that software is compatible with, built with, derived from, or forked from CENTL;
- linking to the official project or discussing it in articles, research, documentation, reviews, presentations, or package metadata;
- retaining legally required notices and attribution;
- accurately identifying an unmodified official CENTL release.

Nominative and other uses permitted by applicable law are not restricted by this policy.

## Forks and modified distributions

The Apache-licensed software may be forked, modified, redistributed, and used commercially. A modified or unofficial distribution must not present itself in a way that is likely to imply that it is an official FCF or CENTL release when it is not.

When a fork makes material product changes, use a clearly differentiated product identity and describe its relationship to CENTL factually, for example: “based on CENTL” or “compatible with CENTL.” Do not use official logos or distinctive release artwork as the fork's primary brand without permission.

## No implied endorsement

Do not use FCF or CENTL branding to imply sponsorship, certification, endorsement, partnership, or official status that has not been granted.

## Relationship to the software license

This policy does not reduce the copyright or patent permissions granted by Apache-2.0. You do not need trademark permission merely to exercise the rights to use, study, modify, redistribute, embed, sell, or fork the Apache-licensed software.

The goal is narrow: **the code stays free while users can still tell what is an official CENTL/FCF release.**
