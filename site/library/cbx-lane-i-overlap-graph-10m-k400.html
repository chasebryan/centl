Exact finite overlap census

The ordered Lane-I cover is highly overlapping, but its late-layer absorption is genuinely collective rather than a trivial small shadow.

Canonical source note in the repository

Finite domain: 20,513 Mordell-hard primes through 10,000,000.

Layers: 100 admissible shifts, k=3,7,...,399.

Relation rows: 534,037 exact standalone hit events.

Actions run: 31929064008 · artifact 9258751000.

Claim boundary: every containment statement is exact only for this finite domain. These are theorem targets, not universal shadow theorems and not a proof of Erdős-Straus.

Ordered novelty reproduces the first-hit frontier

For each layer Hk, define its novelty against all smaller layers:

N_k=H_k\setminus\bigcup_{j<k}H_j.

Exactly 16 layers have nonzero novelty:

3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, 35, 39, 43, 47, 51, 55, 59, 107.

Their novelty counts sum to all 20,513 hard primes, exactly reconstructing the independent first-hit census.

84 layers are shadowed by the prior union

H_k\subseteq\bigcup_{j<k}H_j

holds on the finite domain for 84 of 100 layers. All 73 layers above 107 are in this category.

Yet that union-shadowing does not collapse to an easy small absorber.

No exact one-, two-, or three-layer shadow

The Fedora v2 analyzer exhaustively tested earlier single layers, pairs, and triples using exact bitsets. It found:

\boxed{\text{single}=0,\quad\text{two}=0,\quad\text{three}=0.}

No later finite Hk is contained in one earlier Hj, any union of two earlier layers, or any union of three earlier layers.

This makes the observed absorption genuinely multi-layer on the measured domain.

Strong pairwise overlaps remain

The strongest measured earlier single overlap is H55 against H11:

  • intersection: 8,230 primes;
  • coverage of H55: 78.2245%;
  • Jaccard similarity: 0.5239368.

Other recurring overlap hubs include early layers 11 and 23. They cover roughly 68-72% of several later layers individually, but never 100%.

What this means

The current finite picture is:

\text{large intrinsic layers}+\text{heavy overlap}+\text{no small exact absorber}=\text{collective cover geometry}.

The natural next theorem search is therefore structural multi-layer absorption, especially after conditioning on spectra or congruence classes. The global graph has already ruled out the easiest one-, two-, and three-layer explanation.

Greedy covers are only hints

A greedy earlier-layer cover often uses roughly 6-13 layers for late targets. Those numbers are heuristic and are not minimum set-cover proofs. At k=107 the greedy prior union leaves one hit uncovered, matching the unique first-hit record prime.
