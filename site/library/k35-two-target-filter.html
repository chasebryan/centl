Exact fixed-shift theorem

The composite shift 35 is completely classified by a closed 24-element group-state system.

Canonical research note · Machine certificate

Status: exact computer-assisted finite-group classification. Erdős–Straus remains open.

The state space

For C=(p+35)/4, the unit group modulo 35 is coordinatized as C12 × C2. One prime-valuation occurrence gives the exact transition (D,c) → (D+{0,g,2g}, c+g). Closing under every unit direction exhausts arbitrary factorization residue states without imposing any bound on p.

\boxed{1298\text{ total states},\quad650\text{ hard-center states}}
\boxed{418\text{ hit states},\quad232\text{ combined-miss states}}

Structure of the miss table

All 92 pure Jacobi-subgroup states miss both targets. The remaining 140 misses are non-pure. Every miss state has a state-equivalent core using at most four outside-subgroup valuation units:

minimum outside units   miss states
0                        92
2                       138
4                         2

The two four-unit states are exceptionally thin: each divisor set contains 21 of the 24 group elements, and the Type-I and Type-II targets coincide.

Claim boundary: fixed k=35 is classified. This does not supply a universal finite shift bound or prove Erdős–Straus.
