Claim boundary: these are finite observations. The maximum 107 is not a universal bound, does not establish an adaptive K law, and does not prove Erdős–Straus.

Runtime correction before the formal census

An earlier timeout-driven dry run exposed a signal-handling flaw: a stop signal could arrive inside Lane I and create a partially evaluated final record. That stream was discarded from the formal census. The production CBX build now enters through cbx_runtime.c, completes an entered target atomically, saves sweep/home at the first unprocessed position, and supports deterministic finite runs with --iterations N.

\Gamma=(F,K_I,E_N,A_L)=(11,400,300,400)

Adaptive-K falsification

The analyzer mechanically tests candidate rules against measured first depths and reports their finite failures. On the R stratum, K(p)=ceil(2 log p) fails 244 of 102,502 observed targets. The first failure is p = 2521, where the policy assigns 16 but kI* = 23. The worst deficit is the record p = 8,803,369, where the policy assigns 32 but kI* = 107, a deficit of 75. Surviving the finite corpus would still never upgrade a rule into a theorem.

W solves the target operationally. CBX reveals the independent signed-box and external-NR structures underneath it.

Erdős–Straus remains open. The observed Lane-I maximum 107 is a finite record, not a bound.
