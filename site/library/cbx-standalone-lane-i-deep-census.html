Every admissible Lane-I shift through 399 tested independently against the complete finite Mordell-hard prime universe.

Claim boundary: this measures finite intrinsic layer strength with overlaps allowed. It is not a redundancy theorem, universal K bound, or proof of Erdős-Straus.

The key distinction

The marginal survivor census asks how many targets a shift solves first after all smaller shifts. This standalone census asks how many targets the same shift hits independently.

\text{marginal first-hit contribution}\ne\text{intrinsic layer strength}.

All 100 layers are intrinsically productive

Every admissible shift k=3,7,...,399 hits at least one hard prime independently:

100/100\text{ standalone layers productive}.

More strikingly, every one of the 73 shifts above 107 is productive. So the absence of first-hit layers above 107 in the marginal K400 census is an overlap/order phenomenon, not late-layer emptiness.

The strongest standalone layer

The largest finite intrinsic layer is k=23, hitting 13,860 of 20,513 hard primes, about 67.57%. Other very strong layers include k=47, 11, 31, 71, 39 and 119.

Why 107 is special only marginally

The first-hit census has one finite record prime whose first hit is k=107. But standalone k=107 hits 4,461 hard primes, with spectrum split A=1,499, B=1,494, C=1,468. Its special marginal role comes from cover order and overlap.

Overlap is enormous

The 100 standalone layers produce 534,037 hit events across 20,513 primes. Counting multiplicity, the average hard prime participates in about 26.03 standalone layer hits.

This pushes the next theoretical question toward set containment and shadowing among the intrinsic hit sets H_k, rather than merely asking whether a late layer is nonempty.

Research consequence

A marginally dead layer may still be intrinsically strong because earlier layers stole all of its targets. That makes such layers prime candidates for absorption and shadowing arguments. The safe hybrid planner therefore keeps every shift mathematically required unless a separate proof removes it.
