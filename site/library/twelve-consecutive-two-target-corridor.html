Twelve consecutive integers

3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, 35, 39, 43, 47

are exactly

\boxed{P+1,P+2,\ldots,P+12.}

Each position now has an exact two-target failure language. The late corridor is closed by finite-group state classifiers at 27, 31, 35, 39, 43, and 47.

The sharper cross-shift target at 47

Every hard prime has 6 | (p+47)/4. Once that forced packet is built into the exact state machine, only 196 hard-prime miss states remain at 47, including 80 with (47/p)=-1. Those 80 states reduce to just eleven canonical nonresidue directions modulo 47.

In the 10M corpus, every negative-character k=47 miss is already caught by earlier shifts through 39. The exact finite minimum cover uses seven earlier layers. This is now the leading cross-shift theorem candidate, not a proved containment.

What remains

The preferred theorem target is now to prove that the twelve consecutive exact miss families cannot occur simultaneously. A smaller first step is to prove the observed negative-Legendre exclusion at k=47 from the earlier exact corridor constraints.

Claim boundary: local fixed-shift classifications are exact. The six-prime 10M residual and the negative-Legendre exclusion are finite theorem-hunting evidence. Erdős–Straus remains open.
