Exact finite overlap census

First-hit novelty and standalone layer strength are different mathematical objects.

Canonical repository note

Claim boundary: every number here is finite. Standalone strength does not imply first-hit novelty, and finite containment is not a universal shadow theorem.

All 100 measured layers are productive

The target universe contains 20,513 Mordell-hard primes through 10,000,000. Every admissible shift k=3,7,...,399 hits at least one target when tested independently:

100/100\text{ standalone layers productive.}

There are zero standalone zero-hit layers in this finite domain.

Above 107 is strong-but-shadowed territory

The ordered first-hit cover finishes at k=107. Yet all 73 admissible shifts from 111 through 399 are productive when tested against the full hard-prime universe.

The record-prime gauntlet is not made of weak layers

After k=59, the ordered cover leaves only p=8,803,369. It survives 63,67,...,103 and hits at 107. Standalone testing shows those intermediate layers hit thousands of other hard primes. For example, k=71 hits 12,960 targets, k=95 hits 11,300, and k=103 hits 7,415.

The correct finite interpretation is therefore: strong layers, completely deprived of novelty by the earlier cover, all missing one exceptional survivor.

Overlap is now the research object

The next CBX instrument preserves the exact finite hit set T_k for every shift and computes single-layer containment, two-layer union containment, pairwise overlap and novelty against the smaller-k union. Those relations are theorem candidates, not theorems.
