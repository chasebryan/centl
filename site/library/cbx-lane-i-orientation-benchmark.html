Research library · Erdős–Straus · ES+

Finite benchmark note

Three exact traversals of the same Lane-I signed-box cover, measured on Fedora-family GNU/Linux.

Canonical source note in the repository

Kernel: cbx.kernel 0.1.0

Finite corpus: 2 ≤ p ≤ 100,000, K_I=80, 273 Mordell-hard prime targets.

Environment: Fedora 44 container, GCC 16.1.1.

Claim boundary: exact operation counts are exact only for this stated finite corpus. Wall-clock ratios are machine- and run-specific. Nothing here proves asymptotic superiority, an adaptive K law, or Erdős–Straus.

Three exact orientations

p-major recognition

p \to k \to C=(p+k)/4

C-major construction

k \to C \to p=4C-k

shift-major survivor traversal

k \to p \to C=(p+k)/4

Both non-forward engines independently verify cover membership and minimal first-k against the p-major recognizer. The finite CI corpus has zero mismatches.

Strict C-major baseline

The literal constructive implementation factors every compatible C before target lookup. Its factorization ratio relative to p-major was 20.198020×, and its median three-repeat Fedora wall ratio was 12.190308×.

This falsified the simple idea that reversing the loops is automatically faster.

Target-gated C-major

Cheap exact gates reject generated non-targets, already-covered targets, and non-coprime candidates before factorization. That reduced the expensive factorization ratio to exactly 1.000000× forward.

The traversal still enumerated 20.198020× as many compatible C values as forward performed factorizations, leaving a median wall ratio of 1.325060×.

The remaining penalty is therefore iterator overhead on this finite corpus, not extra signed-box factorization work.

Shift-major survivor frontier

Shift-major keeps k outermost but walks only the unresolved hard-prime frontier. As each target hits, it is removed before the next shift.

The finite regression gate obtains the exact work identities:

\rho_F^{shift}=1.000000, \qquad \rho_V^{shift}=1.000000.

Its median three-repeat Fedora wall ratio was 0.992221× p-major.

Interpretation: practical parity within timing noise. The roughly 0.8% apparent advantage is not treated as a speed theorem.

What changed

The important result is not the tiny timing difference. It is the decomposition of the work:

  • Strict C-major spends expensive arithmetic on too many generated C values.
  • Target gating recovers exactly the p-major expensive factorization work set.
  • Shift-major removes the extra C-enumeration loop while keeping k outermost.

The next question is therefore not simply “forward or inverse?” It is which orientation should own each shift, and what information becomes reusable when all unresolved targets at one k are visible together.

Next algorithmic target

CBX will profile yield and cost per shift, then test a hybrid scheduler:

\text{hybrid Lane I}=\text{generated shifts}+\text{shift-major shifts}+\text{recognition fallback}.

Candidate gates include defect, spectrum, residue and factor-pattern information. Any scheduler remains an empirical engineering policy until separately proved.

Reproduce

make -C research/erdos-straus/cbx.kernel

./centl es cbx inverse --hi 100000 --i-max 80 --segment 25000 --verify
./centl es cbx shift-i --hi 100000 --i-max 80 --segment 25000 --verify

python3 research/erdos-straus/cbx.kernel/bench_i.py \
  --hi 100000 --i-max 80 --repeat 3

Erdős–Straus remains open. These are finite cover algorithms and finite benchmarks, not a proof.
